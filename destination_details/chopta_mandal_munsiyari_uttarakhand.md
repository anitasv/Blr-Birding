## The Landscape

Chopta–Mandal–Munsiyari is a loose circuit through the Garhwal and Kumaon ranges of Uttarakhand, connecting three high-altitude areas that together give access to some of the most beautiful alpine and subalpine birding in India. Chopta sits at 2,700 m, a meadow-and-forest area on the way to the Tungnath temple (the highest Shiva temple in the world), backed by the Kedarnath wildlife sanctuary. Mandal is a quieter valley nearby in the Chamoli district. Munsiyari is a village at 2,200 m in eastern Kumaon, at the foot of the Panchachuli range — a wall of five glaciated peaks that serves as one of the most dramatic mountain backdrops for photography in the Indian Himalaya.

The photography here is about snow peaks, high-altitude birds, and the atmosphere of the upper Himalaya — not safari wildlife.

## Key Species

### Birds

- **Himalayan monal (Impeyan pheasant)** — the national bird of Nepal and the signature bird of the Himalayan high-altitude zone. The male's iridescent plumage covers nearly every colour of the spectrum: metallic greens, blues, copper, and bronze that shift in the high-altitude light. Males display on open slopes from March to May. Chopta and the Tungnath meadows are among the most accessible sites in India for extended monal photography.
- **Koklass pheasant** — lower elevation than monal; in the oak and conifer forest.
- **Western tragopan** — a critically endangered pheasant of the Himalayan forest; extremely secretive but present in the denser temperate forest zones above Mandal.
- **Snow partridge** — the high rocky slopes above the treeline.
- **Wallcreeper** — one of the most sought-after birds in India; a small, brilliantly crimson-and-grey bird that climbs rock faces like a woodpecker. Found on the rocky outcrops and cliff faces around Munsiyari, especially near the Milam glacier approach.
- **Grandala** — a deep-blue thrush of rocky alpine slopes; males in breeding plumage are an intense cobalt.
- **Red-billed chough, yellow-billed chough** — gregarious, aerobatic corvids of the high cliff zones; their tumbling aerial displays against the snow peaks are spectacular.
- **Bearded vulture (lammergeier)** — soars on long, narrow wings over the high ridges; the rust-orange belly and diamond-shaped tail are distinctive at any distance.
- **Golden eagle** — occasional; large and powerful over the ridgelines.
- **Rose finches (multiple species)** — the meadows and scrub hold several rose finch species in winter and spring.

## Photography Notes

Monal photography at Chopta and Tungnath is the primary draw. Males are most visible and behaviourally active from late March to May, during the breeding season when they display on open slopes. The meadows around Tungnath temple — accessible by a 1.5 km walk from the road head at Chopta — are the best location. Arrive before 7 am and wait on the meadow edges.

The combination of snow peak backdrops and birds is the visual language of this region. A 500–600 mm telephoto for the birds, plus a 70–200 mm for environmental shots that include the Himalayan skyline, covers the range well. The light at altitude is exceptional in the post-monsoon period (October–November) — intense, cold, and contrasty in a way that lowland photography rarely achieves.

Munsiyari's value is the Panchachuli backdrop — the five-peaked massif above the village gives landscape compositions that combine glaciated peaks, forest-covered foothills, and the village rooftops. Sunrise from the ridgeline above Munsiyari, with alpenglow on the peaks, is a classic Kumaon photograph.

For wallcreeper, the rocky faces near the Birthi Falls area and the cliff sections on the Milam glacier trek approach are the most reliable zones. It requires patience — watch cliff faces carefully and methodically.
