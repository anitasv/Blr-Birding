## The Landscape

The Great Himalayan National Park (GHNP) in the Kullu district of Himachal Pradesh is a UNESCO World Heritage Site covering 754 sq km of the western Himalayan watershed. The park encompasses four river valleys — Jiwa Nal, Sainj, Tirthan, and Parvati — each cutting deep into the Himalaya from the Kullu valley floor upward to glaciers and permanent snowfields above 5,000 m.

Unlike most Indian wildlife parks, GHNP has no vehicle access into the core area. All photography within the core zone is on foot — multi-day treks into the interior valleys with tents and guides. This is a trekking wilderness rather than a safari destination, and the experience reflects that: solitude, high-altitude landscapes, and birds and animals that have rarely been disturbed by humans.

## Key Species

### Birds

- **Western tragopan** — GHNP is the most important protected area for this critically endangered pheasant. The male is one of the most spectacular birds in Asia: crimson and spotted-white body, with an extraordinary blue-and-red facial wattle display during breeding (April–May) when the male inflates vivid blue wattles around his face and throat. Found in the dense temperate forest at 2,200–3,300 m. Sightings require patience, skilled local guides, and early morning positioning on known territories.
- **Himalayan monal** — present throughout the subalpine zone; the iridescent male is magnificent in the clear high-altitude light.
- **Cheer pheasant** — a large, long-tailed pheasant of rocky grassland and open shrubby slopes; more approachable than the tragopan.
- **Koklass pheasant** — in the temperate forest zone.
- **Snow partridge** — on the rocky alpine zone above 3,500 m.
- **Wallcreeper** — on cliff faces throughout the higher valleys.
- **Lammergeier, Himalayan griffon** — soaring over all elevation zones.
- **White-capped water redstart** — the brilliant red tail of the male flashes at every stream crossing.
- **Plumbeous water redstart** — blue-grey male at fast boulder streams.
- **Blue-capped rock thrush** — on rocky outcrops.

### Mammals

- **Snow leopard** — present in the upper valleys above 3,500 m; rarely seen but GHNP has a resident population. The Tirthan and Sainj upper valleys are the most likely zones.
- **Himalayan brown bear** — the western Himalayan brown bear is a large, powerful animal; encountered occasionally in the subalpine meadows, especially near fruiting berry patches in autumn.
- **Serow** — a large, heavily built goat-antelope of the forested cliffs.
- **Himalayan tahr** — on the rocky grassland-cliff interface in the upper valleys.
- **Bharal (blue sheep)** — in the open alpine zone.

## Photography Notes

GHNP requires a different mindset from every other park on this list. You carry your equipment on foot for multiple days; the photography is entirely opportunistic and immersive. The tradeoff is access to Himalayan landscapes and species that see very few humans.

The Tirthan valley (Gushaini village as the entry point) and the Sainj valley are the most accessible entry routes. Day treks from the valley villages reach good bird habitat; the interior treks of 4–7 days access the best pheasant habitat and alpine wildlife.

For western tragopan specifically, guided sessions from Neoli village (Sainj valley) with guides who know individual territories are the most productive approach. May is peak breeding display season; the male's call — a series of hollow, resonant booms — can be tracked at dawn.

Camera weight matters enormously on multi-day treks. A 500 mm prime on a lightweight carbon tripod covers the key subjects; a 300 mm f/4 is a useful lighter alternative. The high-altitude light is exceptional — clear, cool, with the long-wavelength warmth of dawn at altitude that gives a quality of golden-hour light unavailable at lowland locations.

Access is from Kullu (flight from Delhi) and then road to the valley entry points. The valley rest houses (Forest Department) at Ropa and Sai Ropa are the primary accommodation.
