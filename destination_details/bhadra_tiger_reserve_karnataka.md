## The Landscape

Bhadra Tiger Reserve in the Chikkamagaluru district is one of Karnataka's quieter, less-visited reserves — and that is precisely its appeal. The Bhadra reservoir at the heart of the park creates a mosaic of inundated valleys, reed islands, and submerged forest, surrounded by dense moist deciduous and semi-evergreen forest on the ridges. The Bhadra river and its tributaries thread through the park, creating natural water corridors that concentrate wildlife.

The lack of large resorts and tourist infrastructure means Bhadra has a genuinely wild feel. Wildlife habituates more slowly to vehicles here, which rewards patient photographers who revisit the same spots.

## Key Species

### Mammals

- **Bengal tiger** — present but elusive; the dense forest cover makes sightings less predictable than in Kabini. Camera trap surveys show a healthy population.
- **Asian elephant** — smaller groups than Kabini but regularly seen near the reservoir.
- **Gaur** — common on the forest roads at dawn; the ridgeline roads give beautiful backlighting on the large bulls.
- **Indian leopard** — possibly the mammal most reliably seen here; the rocky outcrops above the forest are classic leopard territory.
- **Sloth bear** — a genuine possibility, especially near fruiting fig trees and termite mounds in the dry months.

### Birds

Bhadra is a serious birding destination and arguably underrated for birds in South India.

- **Malabar pied hornbill** — frequently seen pairs at the forest edge near the reservoir.
- **Great hornbill** — present in the denser western sections; listen for the whooshing wingbeat.
- **Malabar trogon** — one of India's most beautiful birds; the male's crimson belly and dark hood make it unmistakeable. Found in shaded ravines.
- **Heart-spotted woodpecker** — small, striking, found in the tall deciduous forest.
- **White-bellied woodpecker** — India's largest woodpecker; works high on tall dead trees and is often heard before being seen.
- **Crested hawk-eagle, changeable hawk-eagle** — common raptors over forest clearings.
- **Brown-headed barbet, Malabar barbet** — both present; the Malabar barbet is a Western Ghats endemic.
- **Indian paradise flycatcher** — breeding pairs in the forest interior; the male's long white tail ribbons are one of Indian photography's signature images.

## Photography Notes

The boat safari on the Bhadra reservoir is the centrepiece photography experience — flat water, early morning mist, and the chance of close elephant encounters at the water's edge. The boats are small and low, giving excellent eye-level perspectives.

For birding photography, the forest road leading from the Muthodi range office toward the interior is productive in early morning. Trogons favour deep shade so a fast lens (f/2.8–f/4) and high ISO capability matter here. The hornbills are best photographed at fruiting trees — ask your guide about current fruiting locations.

The light quality in Bhadra changes dramatically between seasons. December–January gives crisp, cool light with low humidity; March–May gives warmer tones but the leafless trees help light penetration.

## Getting Around

Entry is through the Muthodi or Lakkavalli ranges. The reserve requires a booked guide and is best visited with a 2-night stay minimum to cover both the boat and forest safari options. The road from Chikkamagaluru is good; from Bengaluru, Shimoga (Shivamogga) is the nearest major town and rail hub.
