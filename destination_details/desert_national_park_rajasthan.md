## The Landscape

Desert National Park near Jaisalmer is the largest national park in India by area — 3,162 sq km of true Thar Desert: sand dunes, flat saline scrub, rocky hammadas, and sparse thornbush. It is the primary habitat in India for the **Great Indian Bustard**, one of the most critically endangered birds in the world, and the only place where a sighting is a realistic possibility.

The desert landscape itself is an extraordinary photography environment. The dunes at Sam and Khuri, the fossil park near Akal, the stark flatness of the saline plains, and the warm ochre-and-gold colour palette of the Thar at different times of day create a visual language that is utterly unlike the forested reserves elsewhere on this list.

## Key Species

### Birds

- **Great Indian Bustard** — fewer than 150 individuals remain in the wild; the Desert National Park is the last stronghold. These are enormous birds — up to 15 kg, standing 1.2 m tall — and the male's display (inflated neck pouch, upright posture, slow strutting) is the most sought-after wildlife photography moment in the region. Sightings require a licensed guide and significant time; the birds are wide-ranging and unpredictable, but the Dibba area within the park is the core zone. The GIB rescue breeding centre near Jaisalmer has recently made captive birds accessible for photography while conservation work continues.
- **Lesser florican** — a smaller bustard species; the male's extraordinary mating leap — launching 2 metres vertically from the grass — is one of Indian wildlife photography's most athletic images. Seasonal presence (monsoon and post-monsoon).
- **MacQueen's bustard** — a winter visitor from Central Asia; present in smaller numbers than historical records.
- **Laggar falcon** — a powerful, locally common raptor of the open desert.
- **Egyptian vulture** — the most common vulture here; the yellow face and white plumage are distinctive.
- **Short-toed snake eagle** — regularly perched on the few elevated points in the flat desert.
- **Desert wheatear, isabelline wheatear** — small ground birds of the rocky areas; both are winter visitors.
- **Variable wheatear** — the male is strikingly patterned black-and-white.
- **Chestnut-bellied sandgrouse, spotted sandgrouse** — the mass morning flights to water are one of the desert's great spectacles.
- **Indian courser** — a long-legged, fast-running bird of the stony desert.

### Mammals

- **Indian desert fox** — one of the more easily photographed canids in India; relatively small and beautiful, it is active in the early morning and late afternoon.
- **Chinkara (Indian gazelle)** — common; the slender legs and rufous coat against the dry landscape are elegant subjects.
- **Desert cat (Indian wild cat)** — rare and nocturnal; occasionally seen at dawn.
- **Blackbuck** — present in the grassier periphery of the park.

## Photography Notes

The Desert National Park divides naturally into two photography approaches: **dune landscapes** and **wildlife**.

For pure landscape photography, the Sam sand dunes 45 km west of Jaisalmer are the classic location, but they are heavily touristed. The quieter dunes further inside the park, accessible with a guide, give undisturbed foreground sand textures. Dawn and dusk are the only times dune photography is meaningful — the oblique light creates the sand-ripple shadows that define the subject.

For wildlife, a good local naturalist who knows the current GIB sightings is essential. Do not attempt GIB photography without an experienced guide; the birds are legally protected and the sightings are known to specialists who track individuals.

Jaisalmer itself — the golden sandstone fort city — is a strong photography subject in its own right. The fort at sunrise and the havelis in the old town reward a dedicated early morning with a wide-angle lens.

The Fossil Park at Akal (10 km from Jaisalmer) has 180-million-year-old fossilised tree trunks from when this desert was a wet forest — an unusual geological photography subject that puts the current landscape in stark perspective.
