## The Landscape

Tal Chhapar is a small grassland sanctuary in the Churu district of Shekhawati, Rajasthan — a flat, almost featureless expanse of short grass and scrub at the edge of the Thar Desert. The sanctuary covers just 71 sq km, but in winter it holds one of the most remarkable concentrations of raptors and grassland birds in India.

The landscape is stark in a way that is visually demanding to photograph well. There are no dramatic rock formations, no lakes, no grand backdrops — just the enormous Rajasthan sky, the pale brown grass, and the birds. Learning to use that sky as your background, and to find the small details of behaviour and character in grassland species, is what Tal Chhapar teaches.

## Key Species

- **Blackbuck** — the primary mammal attraction; the sanctuary supports several hundred individuals. The male blackbuck is one of India's most striking animals — jet black above, white below, with spiralling horns up to 70 cm long. They are active, fast, and group in mixed herds that create excellent herd-and-landscape compositions. The males spar and display from October onwards.
- **Montagu's harrier** — winters here in remarkable numbers. Dozens of harriers — both male and female — quarter low over the grass, hunting. The male's blue-grey plumage against the golden winter light is an exceptional subject.
- **Pallid harrier** — often alongside the Montagu's; a paler, more ghost-like bird.
- **Hen harrier** — less numerous but also present.
- **Short-eared owl** — diurnal during winter; hunts over the grass in the late afternoon light. The flat-faced, streaked owl catching the last light before sunset is one of the most atmospheric images possible at Tal Chhapar.
- **Eastern imperial eagle, tawny eagle, steppe eagle** — large buteonine raptors perching on the few fence posts and low trees around the grassland edge.
- **Laggar falcon** — a large, powerful falcon of the open country.
- **Cream-coloured courser** — a cryptic, fast-running bird of the arid zone; the black-and-white eye stripe and cinnamon-buff plumage are beautifully adapted to the dry grass.
- **Demoiselle crane** — flocks fly over and sometimes land on the grassland; spectacular in flight.
- **Indian sandgrouse** — large flocks fly to water at dawn; the tight, fast-moving flock against the morning sky is a classic desert-edge photography subject.

## Photography Notes

Tal Chhapar is a car-window safari — you drive or walk slowly along the tracks and photograph from vehicle level. For the harriers, which hunt low and approach close, a 400–500 mm is ideal but subjects sometimes pass within 10 metres of the vehicle.

The golden-hour light here is spectacular precisely because of the flatness — the sun sets behind an unobstructed horizon, and the last 30 minutes of light turns the grass, the birds, and the sky into deep gold. Plan to be out on the grassland from 30 minutes before sunset.

The blackbuck benefit from a longer lens (500–600 mm) in the morning when they are spread out, and a shorter one (200–300 mm) when a herd bunches during a chase or courtship display. The spiralling horns and high-contrast coat make the male blackbuck one of the more technically forgiving subjects — the patterns hold detail well even in harsh light.

Combine Tal Chhapar with a Shekhawati painted havelis circuit or a Desert National Park trip for a full Rajasthan photography itinerary.
