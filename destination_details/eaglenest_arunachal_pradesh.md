## The Landscape

Eaglenest Wildlife Sanctuary in the West Kameng district of Arunachal Pradesh is the most species-rich bird area in India — possibly in all of Asia. It sits on the southern slopes of the eastern Himalayas, rising from subtropical foothills at 500 m to temperate forest and rhododendron scrub above 3,000 m. This altitudinal range compresses multiple Himalayan biomes into a single accessible road: subtropical broadleaf forest gives way to warm temperate oak, then cool temperate forest, then subalpine growth as you climb the single road through the sanctuary.

The result is a bird list of over 450 species — including multiple species found nowhere else in the world or accessible nowhere else in India.

This is not a casual destination. It requires a Protected Area Permit (Arunachal Pradesh requires an Inner Line Permit for non-residents), logistically demanding road access, and tolerance for basic camp accommodation. In exchange it offers a birding and photography experience that has no parallel on the subcontinent.

## Key Species

### Endemic and Highly Sought Birds

- **Bugun liocichla** — one of the rarest birds in the world; discovered as a new species at Eaglenest in 2006 and found nowhere else. Small, with a vivid mosaic of colours. The site near Lama Camp where the type specimen was described is the primary location; sightings are not guaranteed and require patience.
- **Ward's trogon** — a large, spectacular trogon of the temperate forest; the male has crimson underparts and a pale bill. Found in the 2,000–2,800 m zone.
- **Beautiful nuthatch** — arguably the most visually stunning bird in Eaglenest; the male's cobalt-blue back and black-and-white face is extraordinary. Found on large-diameter mossy trunks in the temperate forest.
- **Blyth's kingfisher** — a large, forest kingfisher of fast streams; rarely seen well elsewhere.
- **Rufous-necked hornbill** — the most spectacular hornbill of the eastern Himalayas; the male's chestnut head and neck, black body, and massive yellow bill make it unmistakeable.
- **Great hornbill** — also present at lower elevations.
- **Temminck's tragopan** — a pheasant of breathtaking beauty; the male's orange and crimson plumage, with electric-blue facial wattles, is among the most extraordinary in the bird world. Found in the temperate zone in early spring (March–April).
- **Blyth's tragopan, Satyr tragopan** — both also present at different elevations.
- **Spotted wren-babbler, Streaked wren-babbler** — near-impossible to see well, but present in the bamboo understorey.
- **Long-tailed broadbill** — the fluorescent yellow crown and blue wings flash in flight through the subtropical forest.

### Mammals

- **Red panda** — present in the temperate bamboo-oak forest; rarely seen but possible in the Eaglenest high zones.
- **Himalayan black bear** — present; occasionally seen on the higher road sections.
- **Capped langur, Assamese macaque** — common in the subtropical zone.

## Photography Notes

Eaglenest's photography is exclusively bird and forest photography — this is not a mammal destination. The key is working the altitudinal gradient systematically over multiple days.

The subtropical zone (Bomdi La road to the lower gate): long-tailed broadbill, hornbills, and a vast array of babblers and flycatchers. Mornings before 8 am are the peak activity window.

The temperate zone (Lama Camp area at ~2,100 m): the Bugun liocichla territory, beautiful nuthatch, Ward's trogon, and the tragopans. This zone requires patience and quiet; loud groups and constant vehicle movement reduce sightings sharply.

A 500–600 mm telephoto is essential. Many of the key species are in dense canopy or bamboo, and light levels in the forest are low — a lens with f/4 or better maximum aperture is important. High ISO performance (ISO 6400+) matters here more than anywhere else on this list.

The access road itself is the primary photography trail — there is no formal trail network. Position the vehicle on a good forest section before dawn and work the birds as they become active.

Inner Line Permits for Arunachal Pradesh should be arranged well in advance through a licensed tour operator based in the state; independent entry is not permitted.
