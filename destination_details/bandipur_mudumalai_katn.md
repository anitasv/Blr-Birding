## The Landscape

Bandipur and Mudumalai are contiguous reserves straddling the Karnataka-Tamil Nadu border, part of the Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve — the largest protected forest complex in South Asia. The terrain is dry deciduous: wide, open valleys of dry teak and thorny undergrowth, punctuated by rocky ridgelines and seasonal streams. In the dry months the forest floor turns gold-brown, and the open sightlines make for clean, uncluttered mammal shots that are harder to get in the denser forests of Kabini.

The Moyar river marks the boundary between the two reserves and is a reliable wildlife corridor. Both parks share the same elephant population, and crossing herds between the Karnataka and Tamil Nadu sides are a regular sight from the highway bridge.

## Key Species

### Mammals

- **Asian elephant** — some of India's largest herds move through this landscape. The open dry forest means you can often see 40–60 elephants in a single frame. Watch for herds at the Gopalaswamy Betta reservoir area (Bandipur) just after dawn.
- **Gaur** — abundant and often spectacularly lit on open ridgelines in early morning.
- **Bengal tiger** — present across both parks; sightings are real but the thick undergrowth in wetter areas can be frustrating. The Masinagudi side of Mudumalai tends to produce better tiger sightings on the open jeep tracks.
- **Indian leopard** — widespread and nocturnal; best chances are very early or late safaris.
- **Chital (spotted deer)** — the most abundant large mammal; golden-coated against dry red earth in summer makes for beautiful light.
- **Sambar** — large and often solitary; look for them near water.

### Birds

- **Indian peafowl** — extraordinarily common; males display from February onward, giving extended photography windows.
- **Grey junglefowl** — the wild ancestor of the domestic chicken; males are strikingly patterned and found at forest edges in the early morning.
- **Crested serpent eagle** — frequently heard and seen soaring over valley clearings.
- **Changeable hawk-eagle** — a compact, aggressive raptor; perches prominently at forest edges.
- **Indian roller** — dazzling turquoise-and-chestnut flash in flight; common on open perches.
- **Malabar giant squirrel** — not a bird but visually spectacular; rust-and-black coat in the high canopy.

## Photography Notes

Bandipur's main safari zone uses open-topped buses (gypsies are now restricted in the core), which limits you to fixed positions on the vehicle. Bring a beanbag. The bus format actually works well for elephant herds — the height gives you a view over the scrub and lets you frame herds against the landscape.

Mudumalai's Masinagudi zone allows private jeeps and is better for flexible positioning. The open thorn scrub here gives clean backgrounds and long sight-lines — ideal for 500–600 mm work on tigers and leopards.

Summer light (April–May) is harsh at midday but the golden hour windows before 7:30 am and after 5 pm are superb. The dry, leafless trees mean light reaches the forest floor — a rare opportunity in Indian reserves.

## Getting Around

Bandipur's main gate is at Mangala village; book forest department morning and afternoon safaris well in advance. Mudumalai's Theppakkadu range is the main entry. The highway (NH 766) between Mysuru and Ooty cuts through both parks — night driving is banned, which keeps the park road relatively undisturbed.
