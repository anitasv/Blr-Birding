## The Landscape

Tadoba-Andhari Tiger Reserve in the Chandrapur district of Maharashtra is consistently rated among the top two or three parks in India for tiger photography — and it earns that reputation. The forest is dry deciduous teak, with the Tadoba and Andhari rivers and several lakes creating a network of permanent water that tigers, leopards, and gaur rely on through the long summer heat.

The key to Tadoba's photographic reputation is the combination of open, relatively sparse forest cover (which allows clear sightlines) and a high density of well-habituated tigers that regularly use the lakeshores and riverbanks — settings that provide dramatic, uncluttered backgrounds impossible in denser forest.

## Key Species

### Mammals

- **Bengal tiger** — Tadoba currently holds around 80–90 tigers in a relatively small area, giving it one of the highest sighting rates of any Indian reserve. The Tadoba lake and Andhari river zones are the centrepieces — tigers come to drink and cool off in the heat, often remaining visible at the water's edge for extended periods. The Moharli and Kolsa zones on the western side are less visited but equally productive.
- **Indian leopard** — the most underrated subject at Tadoba. The leopard population here is large and the dry, open forest means they are seen on the roads with far more frequency than at other tiger-dominated parks. Early morning drives on the buffer zone roads can produce multiple leopard encounters before the tiger activity begins.
- **Indian wild dog (dhole)** — packs are regularly seen; the lakeside encounters between dhole and tiger are some of the most dramatic predator-predator interactions in Indian wildlife.
- **Gaur** — massive herds in the buffer zone teak forest.
- **Sloth bear** — present; the forest areas between Moharli and Navegaon gate are the better zones.
- **Sambar, chital, nilgai** — abundant prey base.

### Birds

- **Crested serpent eagle, changeable hawk-eagle** — both common raptors.
- **Indian roller** — a perch bird on every open track.
- **Grey-headed fish eagle** — at Tadoba lake; a large, imposing raptor.
- **Lesser adjutant stork** — a rare and tall stork; occasional visitors to Tadoba lake.
- **Wooly-necked stork** — at the water margins.
- **Brown fish owl** — roosts in large fig trees overhanging the lake; found by scanning at dusk.

## The Lakes as Photography Sets

Tadoba lake and Kolsa lake function as natural photography sets. Both have clear, open banks where tigers, leopards, and gaur approach water predictably. The lake surface reflects the sky and the animals drinking, adding visual depth to images. The surrounding teak forest provides clean, out-of-focus brown and gold backgrounds when shooting at wide aperture.

In the summer months (April–May), the lakes are the only reliable water sources and the traffic of animals at the banks accelerates dramatically. A full morning session at Tadoba lake in April can produce 4–6 tiger sightings — uncommon at any other park.

## Photography Notes

Zone selection at Tadoba significantly affects experience quality. The Moharli gate and Tadoba gate zones (northern section) cover the main lake and the highest-density tiger zone. The Kolsa gate zone (south) is less known but the Kolsa lake produces excellent encounters in a less crowded setting. Buffer zone safaris (available separately from the core) are excellent for leopard photography.

April and May are brutally hot but photographically extraordinary — 35–45°C temperatures concentrate every animal at water. Early morning departures (5:30 am) are essential to be at the lake edge before the heat builds.

November–March gives comfortable temperatures and good sighting rates; December and January see more activity throughout the day as the tigers are less heat-stressed.

A 500–600 mm is standard. The Tadoba lake reflections work well with a wider 300 mm that includes water and treeline alongside the subject.
