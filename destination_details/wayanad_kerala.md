## The Landscape

Wayanad is a highland plateau at 700–2,100 m in northern Kerala, sharing borders with both Karnataka (Nagarhole) and Tamil Nadu (Mudumalai). The landscape is a mix of coffee, tea, and cardamom plantations interspersed with remnant forest fragments and the two main protected areas — Muthanga and Tholpetty wildlife sanctuaries. The Kabini river rises in the Wayanad plateau before flowing down into the Nagarhole reservoir.

Wayanad is less intensively managed for tourism than Kabini, which means fewer guaranteed sightings but a more relaxed, exploratory atmosphere. The plantation roads, forest edges, and tribal village vicinities create excellent birdwatching conditions throughout the district, not just within the formal protected areas.

## Key Species

### Mammals

- **Asian elephant** — Wayanad has one of the highest human-elephant conflict rates in India, which is a reminder that elephants are genuinely everywhere here. Large breeding herds move through the coffee estates, road corridors, and the reserve buffer zones. The Tholpetty range and the Thirunelli forest road are the most reliable zones.
- **Gaur** — regularly seen on the road between Kalpetta and Sultan Bathery (Sulthan Batheri) in the early morning.
- **Indian leopard** — common in the buffer zones; the coffee estate edges are good leopard habitat.
- **Lion-tailed macaque** — the forest patches between Wayanad and the Nilgiris hold troops.

### Birds

- **Malabar grey hornbill** — the Western Ghats endemic, common in the forest corridors.
- **Great hornbill** — present in the taller forest sections near Muthanga.
- **Malabar trogon** — in the shadier interior forest.
- **Orange minivet** — flocks of the male's vivid orange-and-black plumage move through the canopy; the forest lights up when a flock passes.
- **Asian fairy-bluebird** — the male is an extraordinary deep blue and black; seen in fruiting trees in the forest interior.
- **Malabar whistling thrush** — along every stream.
- **Heart-spotted woodpecker** — in the tall deciduous forest sections.

## Photography Notes

Wayanad works best as a **landscape and incidental wildlife** destination rather than a focused safari trip. The plantation roads give an open-vehicle birding experience at low cost — drive slowly through coffee estate roads in the early morning and treat the whole landscape as your photography zone.

The Thirunelli temple road through the forest is one of the most scenic drives in Kerala; the ancient temple itself, set in a valley of tall forest with a rocky stream, is a strong landscape photography subject.

Tholpetty range jeep safaris are government-operated and reliable for elephant and gaur sightings in the north. Muthanga is closer to Sultan Bathery but has had more human-wildlife conflict at the boundary. Book Tholpetty safaris in advance.

The plantation landscape photography here — orderly rows of coffee under shade trees, mist in the valley at dawn, tribal communities in the buffer zone — is underexplored and has genuine documentary potential.
