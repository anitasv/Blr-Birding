## The Landscape

Namdapha National Park in Changlang district is one of the largest protected areas in India and one of its least visited. It spans an extraordinary altitudinal range — from 200 m at the Noa-Dehing river valley to over 4,500 m at the peaks on the Myanmar border — encompassing tropical rainforest, temperate broadleaf forest, alpine meadows, and snowfields within a single contiguous protected area.

The Noa-Dehing river is the park's artery: a fast, boulder-strewn river flowing through a valley of extraordinary forest that has never been commercially logged. The tall, multilayered lowland rainforest here — with emergent dipterocarps, strangler figs, and a dense understorey — is among the finest in South Asia.

Namdapha has the distinction of supporting all four large wild cats of India within a single park: tiger, leopard, clouded leopard, and snow leopard. In practice, seeing any of them is unlikely. The value here is the forest itself and its birds.

## Key Species

### Birds

- **White-winged duck** — one of the world's most endangered ducks; a large, dark forest duck of quiet backwaters and oxbow lakes in lowland rainforest. Namdapha is one of the few remaining sites. Dawn visits to the Noa-Dehing backwaters are the best opportunity.
- **Rufous-necked hornbill** — spectacular; the chestnut-red head and neck of the male is vivid against the dark forest.
- **Great hornbill, wreathed hornbill** — both present in the lowland forest.
- **Long-tailed broadbill** — flocks in the subtropical zone; the iridescent blue-and-yellow pattern is visible even in dense canopy.
- **Blue-naped pitta** — a ground bird of the forest floor; extraordinarily vivid blue, green, and red plumage but cryptic behaviour.
- **Blyth's kingfisher, crested kingfisher** — along the Noa-Dehing and its tributaries.
- **Pale-headed woodpecker** — a large woodpecker of the mixed forest.
- **Sultan tit** — the world's largest tit; crested, yellow, and black, found in the canopy.

### Mammals

- **Hoolock gibbon** — calls echo across the Noa-Dehing valley at dawn; the song is one of the most haunting natural sounds in India.
- **Binturong (bearcat)** — nocturnal, arboreal, and extremely rarely seen; the strong-smelling prehensile tail and bearlike face make it one of Asia's most distinctive mammals.
- **Stump-tailed macaque** — found in the lower forest zones.
- **Asian elephant** — present in the valley lowlands.
- **Clouded leopard** — present; photographed only on camera traps.

## Photography Notes

Namdapha's photography is forest immersion photography. The infrastructure is limited to the Deban range office and a small guesthouse; most serious visits involve camping along the river or at established forest department camps.

The Noa-Dehing river valley, walkable from Deban, is the core photography area. Dawn walks along the riverbank, before the forest becomes too active and loud for close observation, give the best bird photography opportunities. The white-winged duck requires pre-dawn positioning at known backwater sites with a local guide.

For general forest bird photography, a 500 mm telephoto with fast autofocus performs far better in this light-limited environment than a slower, longer lens. The forest canopy filters most direct light, and action sequences — hornbills flying between canopy trees — require shutter speeds of 1/1000s or faster to freeze wing movement.

Travel to Namdapha requires flying to Dibrugarh (Assam) and then a long road journey via Miao. An Inner Line Permit is required well in advance. Organise logistics through a Miao-based tour operator or through the Arunachal Forest Department.

The experience here is expedition wildlife photography — go for the forest, the river, and the birds of the lowland rainforest, not for mammal sightings.
