## The Landscape

Vedanthangal and Pulicat are two very different wetland destinations that pair well together on a trip from Bengaluru to Chennai or the Tamil Nadu coast.

**Vedanthangal** is India's oldest protected bird sanctuary — a small, ancient tank (reservoir) that has been a waterbird breeding site for centuries. Local farmers have protected it for generations, recognising that the droppings from the nesting colony fertilise their fields. The result is one of the most intense and accessible breeding waterbird colonies in Asia: a single 30-hectare tank supporting tens of thousands of nesting birds.

**Pulicat** is the second-largest saltwater lake in India, a shallow coastal lagoon separated from the Bay of Bengal by a narrow spit. It is the most important flamingo site in South India, and in good water-level years, tens of thousands of lesser flamingos turn the lake pink from a distance.

## Key Species

### Vedanthangal

- **Spot-billed pelican** — Vedanthangal is one of the most important breeding sites for this species in India; dozens of nesting pairs build flat stick nests in the trees above the water.
- **Painted stork** — present in large numbers; breeding adults develop a pink flush on the lower back.
- **Asian openbill** — nests in dense colonies.
- **Black-headed ibis, glossy ibis** — both present; the glossy ibis's metallic bronze-green sheen in direct light is stunning.
- **Night herons (black-crowned and rufous)** — Vedanthangal has both species; the rufous night heron is less common and more sought-after.
- **Little egret, intermediate egret** — in the hundreds; the white plumage on the dark water surface makes for graphic, high-contrast images.
- **Garganey, pintail, shoveler** — winter ducks arrive on the tank from October.

### Pulicat

- **Lesser flamingo** — the primary draw; numbers vary enormously by year and season (November–January is peak). In a good year, 30,000+ birds turn the shallows pink. Their feeding motion — sweeping the bill upside-down through the water — is endlessly interesting to watch and photograph.
- **Greater flamingo** — present alongside the lesser in smaller numbers; a full head taller, with a pink-tipped bill and paler plumage.
- **Painted stork, pelican, cormorant** — all at Pulicat in good numbers.
- **Bar-headed goose** — occasional visitor; notable for being one of the highest-altitude migrating birds in the world, crossing the Himalayas.
- **Sandpipers, plovers, stilts** — the extensive mudflats support large wader flocks in winter.

## Photography Notes

At Vedanthangal, the colony photography happens from the bund (embankment) running along the tank edge. You are 15–40 metres from the nearest trees, which are packed with nesting birds. A 400–500 mm lens is ideal, but a 300 mm will also work on the denser trees. The best light is the first 90 minutes after sunrise when the low angle lights the nesting birds from the side.

Peak breeding is usually November–January. Come early — the car park fills by 7 am on weekends.

At Pulicat, the flamingos are typically in the centre of the lake and require a boat to approach closely. Local fishermen near the Pulicat town jetty can be hired. A 500–600 mm lens is worth bringing. The quality of the pink light on flamingo flocks at dawn — with the pale sky reflected in the shallow water — is one of Tamil Nadu's finest photography opportunities. Water levels vary by year; check current conditions before visiting.
