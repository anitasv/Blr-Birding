## The Landscape

Keoladeo Ghana National Park — universally known as Bharatpur — is a flat, man-made wetland that was originally a duck-hunting reserve for the Maharajas of Bharatpur. The 29 sq km park is a mosaic of shallow lakes, marshes, dry grassland, and scrub forest, all connected by a grid of elevated paths and tracks passable on foot, bicycle, or cycle-rickshaw.

The combination of flatness, open visibility, and a network of paths that put you at water level with the birds makes Bharatpur unique in Indian birding. You are not looking up at birds in trees — you are eye-level with herons in the reeds, ducks on the water, and raptors on low perches. This translates directly into photograph quality: clean backgrounds, eye contact, and natural behaviour.

## Key Species

### Resident and Breeding Birds

- **Painted stork** — nests in the park in large numbers (November onward); the large, pink-flushed adults and young chicks in the nests are classic Bharatpur subjects.
- **Spot-billed pelican** — present year-round; enormous and strange, they rest on the low vegetation and fish in groups.
- **Asian openbill, woolly-necked stork** — both breeding.
- **Darter, little cormorant, Indian cormorant** — roosting in the trees at the Python point area.
- **Purple heron, grey heron, night heron** — in the reeds and the tall acacia trees.
- **Sarus crane** — the world's tallest flying bird; pairs are resident on the grassland areas. The Sarus crane is revered in Indian culture and Bharatpur has one of the most reliably accessible populations in North India. Watch for the dancing courtship display.

### Winter Migrants (October–March)

- **Bar-headed goose** — the high-altitude Himalayan migrant; flocks arrive from October.
- **Greylag goose, lesser white-fronted goose** — regular.
- **Common teal, gadwall, pintail, shoveler, pochard** — ducks fill the lakes in winter in the thousands.
- **Ferruginous pochard** — a globally vulnerable diving duck; Bharatpur is one of the more reliable sites in India.
- **Osprey** — hovers and dives on the larger channels; a spectacular action subject against the flat water.
- **Greater spotted eagle, imperial eagle** — winter raptors; the imperial eagle is one of the more sought-after birds among visiting birders.
- **Siberian crane** — historically the great draw to Bharatpur; the last confirmed sightings were in 2002. Their absence is a conservation story worth knowing.

## Photography Notes

Bharatpur's flat, open paths make it the most **light-efficient** birding location in India. At dawn, the low sun skims across the water surface and lights the birds from the side at near-perfect angles. The cycle-rickshaws allow silent approach to within a few metres of feeding birds in the water.

A 400–500 mm covers most of the work, but the cycle-rickshaw-based photography at close range also rewards a 300 mm or even a 70–200 mm for environmental shots that include the water surface and reeds.

The grassland areas in the southern section of the park hold Sarus cranes, painted sandgrouse, and raptors — a different but equally valuable photography zone from the wetlands. Early morning visits to these areas, separate from the main wetland circuit, are worth planning.

November to January is peak season: the migrant ducks are present, the stork colony is at its most active, and the cool weather makes the long walks comfortable. February onwards the ducks start departing; March is the last good month before the summer heat.
