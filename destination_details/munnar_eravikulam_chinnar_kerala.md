## The Landscape

The Munnar region spans three distinct protected areas with very different characters. Eravikulam National Park is high-altitude grassland and shola forest, home to the largest population of the **Nilgiri tahr**. Chinnar Wildlife Sanctuary to the northeast is a rain-shadow dry forest, dryer and thornier than anything in the wet western districts. The town of Munnar itself sits at 1,600 m, surrounded by some of the most extensive tea estates in India.

The ecological contrast within a single trip makes this region unusual — you can photograph alpine grassland mammals in the morning and visit arid thornbush in the afternoon.

## Key Species

### Mammals

- **Nilgiri tahr** — a stocky, dark-coated mountain ungulate related to wild goats. Eravikulam has the world's largest population (around 750–900 animals), and they are remarkably tame near the Rajamala entrance. Herds of 20–30 tahr feeding on the rolling grassland, with the blue hills behind, is the signature image of this region. Males have a distinctive saddle-patch of pale grey on the back.
- **Gaur** — large bulls are occasionally seen crossing the road between Munnar and Eravikulam.
- **Asian elephant** — moved between the various forest blocks; more often encountered on the road between Munnar and Top Station than in the park itself.
- **Spotted deer, sambar** — present in Chinnar's drier sections.
- **Indian star tortoise** — Chinnar is one of the best places in India to see this beautiful tortoise in the wild; the geometric shell pattern is extraordinary.
- **Grizzled giant squirrel** — a Critically Endangered squirrel known from Chinnar; the combination of grey-grizzled coat and the dry forest setting is unique.

### Birds

- **Nilgiri wood pigeon** — a large, handsome endemic pigeon of the shola forest; the scaled pattern on the neck is distinctive.
- **Black-and-orange flycatcher** — a striking endemic of the shola edges in Eravikulam.
- **Nilgiri flycatcher** — the male is an intense dark blue; found in the shola-grassland ecotone.
- **Broad-tailed grassbird** — a skulking bird of tall grassland; the high-altitude grasslands of Eravikulam are the best place to look.
- **Yellow-browed bulbul** — common and vocal at mid-elevation forest edges.
- **Hill swallow** — sweeps over the open grasslands.
- **Painted bush-quail** — a beautiful endemic quail of open grassland.

## Photography Notes

Eravikulam's tahr are the easiest large mammal photographic subject in the Western Ghats — they approach to within a few metres near the main entrance area and are completely habituated to people. A 100–300 mm lens is sufficient for portrait shots. For landscape-scale images of herds against the rolling grass and hills, a 70–200 mm works well.

The shola forest patches at the grassland edges hold the endemic birds. Early morning, before tourists arrive, gives the quietest conditions. The Rajamala road and the upper viewpoints are the most productive zones.

Chinnar has a very different atmosphere — drier, quieter, and more remote-feeling. The star tortoises are found by slow walking on the dry rocky ground with a guide. The grizzled giant squirrel is active in the large trees near the forest department quarters.

Tea estate landscapes around Munnar are worth a dedicated morning for landscape photography — the geometric rows of tea bushes on curved hillsides, with mist filling the valleys and the blue Ghats behind, is a classic South India scene. Best between 6 and 9 am before the haze builds.
