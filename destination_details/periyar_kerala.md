## The Landscape

Periyar Tiger Reserve is built around the Periyar Lake — a large artificial reservoir created in 1895 by the Mullaperiyar Dam. The lake sits at 900 m inside the reserve, surrounded by dense tropical forest. Boat safaris on the lake are the primary wildlife experience, and on a calm morning the water reflects the forested hills perfectly, creating a symmetrical, painterly quality to the landscape.

The reserve is one of the most visited in South India, which has driven the development of excellent infrastructure — well-organised boat safaris, comfortable lodges both inside and outside the park, and experienced guides. It is not a wilderness experience in the way that Kabini or Bhadra are, but for a first India safari, the combination of comfort, accessibility, and near-certain elephant sightings makes it a strong choice.

## Key Species

### Mammals

- **Asian elephant** — the dominant wildlife experience at Periyar. Herds of 10–30 elephants come to the lake shore daily to drink and bathe. From the boat, you are level with the animals at the water's edge — a very different perspective from most safari vehicles. Watch for bulls in the water, mothers with young calves, and the occasional dramatic territorial display between males.
- **Gaur** — large groups, especially on the grassy banks of the lake's northern arms.
- **Sambar** — regularly seen at the water's edge.
- **Wild boar** — common along the forest margins.
- **Otter** — smooth-coated otter families are seen in the lake's quieter channels; they fish in coordinated groups and are one of the more actively behavioural mammal subjects in India.
- **Nilgiri langur** — the black-coated langur of the Western Ghats; troops move through the shoreline trees.

### Birds

- **Darter, cormorant** — roosting on dead branches projecting from the lake.
- **Grey-headed fish eagle** — large and imposing; regularly perched in the tall trees at the lake edge.
- **Crested serpent eagle** — soars and calls over the open water.
- **Malabar grey hornbill** — in the forest along the shore.
- **Painted bat** — roosts in clusters in dense vegetation; bright orange and black, astonishing for a bat.

## Photography Notes

The boat safari at Periyar has one structural limitation: you are on a fixed-route large boat with other tourists, which limits positioning flexibility. Despite this, the close approach to elephants at the waterline is often exceptional — 30 metres from a bathing herd with the reflection in the water is hard to replicate anywhere else.

The best photography comes from the early morning departure (usually 7:30 am). Get a position on the bow of the boat before it fills up. A 300–400 mm covers most of the distances well; a wider lens for the lake-and-forest landscape compositions is worth having along.

The bamboo raft safaris (book through the Forest Department) go deeper into the reserve and give a much quieter, more immersive experience than the main boat. These are limited to small groups and require advance booking.

The forest around Kumily town outside the reserve has good birding — the altitude ensures Western Ghats endemics are accessible on a pre-dawn walk along the road toward the reserve entrance.
