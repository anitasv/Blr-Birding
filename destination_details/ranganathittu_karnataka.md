## The Landscape

Ranganathittu is a compact cluster of rocky islands on the Cauvery river, about 16 km from Mysuru. What makes it exceptional is not the size — the sanctuary covers just 0.67 sq km — but the density. During the breeding season (June–November) the islands are packed with nesting waterbirds to a degree that is genuinely hard to believe. Trees drip with painted storks, open-billed storks, spoonbills, herons, cormorants, and ibis, all nesting within metres of the next colony.

Outside breeding season, the bird numbers reduce but the resident species remain, and the flat-bottomed boat safari on the river gives consistent, close-range access to waterbirds throughout the cooler months.

## Key Species

- **Painted stork** — the flagship species; large, pink-flushed, with an extraordinary yellow bill and black chest band. Nesting adults and chicks from July onward are world-class photography subjects at very close range from the boat.
- **Eurasian spoonbill** — flocks rest and feed along the shallow channels; the spatula bill is fascinating for behavioural shots of feeding.
- **Black-headed ibis** — nests colonially on the islands; the curved bill and gleaming white plumage make for clean portrait shots.
- **Asian openbill** — a smaller stork with a bizarre, gap-billed appearance; eats freshwater snails and specialises in prying them open.
- **Little egret, intermediate egret, grey heron** — all common, all at various stages of breeding plumage from January through monsoon.
- **Indian cormorant, little cormorant** — abundant roost birds.
- **River tern** — nests on exposed rocks in the river; hovers and dives, giving flight photography opportunities.
- **Common kingfisher** — reliably perched on low branches over the water; one of the most satisfying subjects for flight/dive sequences.
- **Pied kingfisher** — the hovering kingfisher; a completely different hunting technique and excellent for action photography.
- **Mugger crocodile** — large individuals sunbathe on the island banks, unconcerned by the boats. Excellent for detail shots — texture of scales, eye close-ups.
- **Indian flying fox** — vast colonies roost in the trees; the leathery wings folded in sleep, or the dramatic departure at dusk, are both compelling subjects.

## Photography Notes

The boat safari is the entire experience here, and it is remarkably good for a place this close to a city. The boatmen are experienced and will hold position near birds patiently. Bring a 400–500 mm for birds on the islands, a 70–200 mm for close flyovers, and a wide-angle for the colony scenes and river environment.

Breeding season (June–November) is the peak for colony photography — nesting behaviour, chick feeding, territorial disputes, courtship displays. Outside this, November–February is better for non-breeding plumage and general birding, with lower humidity and better light quality.

Early morning is essential — the soft light across the river before 8 am is far superior to the harsh midday glare. The river surface reflects the sky beautifully in morning calm before the wind picks up.

A half-day visit is plenty for the boat safari, which makes Ranganathittu an easy add-on to a Mysuru trip or the start of a Kabini drive.
