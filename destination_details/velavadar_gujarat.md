## The Landscape

Velavadar National Park is a small (34 sq km), very flat grassland sanctuary on the Saurashtra coast of Gujarat. The grass is short and golden in winter, the ground is pancake-flat, and the light arrives unobstructed from horizon to horizon. It is a one-habitat park, and the photography is correspondingly focused — but within that focused scope, the subjects are spectacular.

Velavadar is the best place in the world to photograph **blackbuck** in the wild. The sanctuary holds around 4,000 animals in a density that means herds of hundreds are routine. It is also one of the most reliable sites in India for **wolf** — small packs hunt the grassland by day and can be followed for extended periods.

## Key Species

### Mammals

- **Blackbuck** — the dominant subject. Adult males develop jet-black upperparts and spiral horns only after 3 years; the contrast between glossy black and white is extraordinary in good light. The large mixed herds — males displaying and chasing, females and young at the edges — provide constant behavioural photography material. During the rut (October–February), males spar vigorously and hold territories on the open grass; the territorial displays — neck arched, horns tilted back, feet stamping — are superbly photogenic.
- **Indian wolf** — Velavadar has a pack of 4–8 wolves that has been resident for years. These are Indian wolves (smaller than European wolves, with a paler coat), and they hunt blackbuck on the open grassland. Following a wolf hunt on flat ground with good visibility is one of the most sustained wildlife photography sequences possible in India. Dawn and dusk are the most active periods.
- **Nilgai** — present on the edges of the grassland.
- **Indian fox** — common; often very approachable in the early morning.
- **Jungle cat** — occasionally seen in the taller grass areas.

### Birds

- **Montagu's harrier** — winters in large numbers; the male's pale grey plumage and black wingtips, hunting low over the grass in the golden afternoon light, are a classic Velavadar image.
- **Pallid harrier** — alongside the Montagu's; the near-white adult males are ghostly.
- **Short-eared owl** — hunts by day in winter; perches on the small acacia trees in the grassland.
- **Indian coursers** — fast, long-legged birds of the open ground; cryptically patterned.
- **Lesser florican** (summer) — a small bustard with a remarkable vertical mating leap; the males jump repeatedly from the grass to advertise themselves.
- **Sarus crane** — pairs on the agricultural edges around the park.
- **Indian sandgrouse** — flocks fly to water before 8 am.

## Photography Notes

The flat terrain at Velavadar means that the horizon is your primary compositional element. Low camera positions — lying prone, or shooting from a vehicle floor — are essential for avoiding that flat horizon cutting through your subject's legs. A low shooting position also creates compression between near and far blackbuck in a herd, amplifying the sense of density.

Wolf photography at Velavadar is best done by following the pack with a guide who knows their territory. The pack's daily circuit can be predictable; good guides can anticipate when a hunt is building. For following action sequences, a 500 mm on a monopod in the open vehicle gives speed and reach.

The grassland at golden hour turns from gold to deep amber, and a herd of blackbuck silhouetted against the setting sun is a consistent, reliable shot in this park. The flat sky also means that overcast days produce beautiful, even light with no harsh shadows — underrated for detailed portrait work on blackbuck males.
