## The Landscape

The Pangot–Sattal–Corbett corridor in Uttarakhand covers three distinct photography environments linked by proximity. Pangot is a small village 15 km from Nainital in the Kumaon hills, sitting at around 2,000 m in oak and rhododendron forest. Sattal (Seven Lakes) is a cluster of freshwater lakes in the valley below Bhimtal, surrounded by dense subtropical and lower temperate forest. Jim Corbett National Park is the centrepiece — India's oldest national park, covering the valley of the Ramganga river in the Terai foothills.

Together they give a Himalayan foothills birding trip that transitions from high-altitude Himalayan species at Pangot down through the subtropical forest at Sattal to the river and grassland wildlife of Corbett — covering more ecological variety than almost any other combination of sites in northern India.

## Key Species

### Pangot (Himalayan Species)

- **Khalij pheasant** — common at forest edges in the early morning; the male's white-flecked black plumage and red facial skin are distinctive.
- **Koklass pheasant** — in the oak-rhododendron forest; vocal at dawn.
- **Himalayan woodpecker** — a large, striking black-and-white woodpecker of the temperate forest.
- **White-throated laughingthrush, Striated laughingthrush** — noisy, social babblers of the undergrowth.
- **Rufous-bellied niltava** — the male's vivid cobalt-and-orange plumage is a standout in the forest understorey.
- **Grey-winged blackbird** — a Himalayan thrush; the grey wings contrast with a warm rufous underpart.
- **Bar-tailed treecreeper** — on the bark of large oaks, working upward in spirals.

### Sattal (Subtropical Forest)

- **Blue-bearded bee-eater** — a large, spectacular bee-eater; the electric turquoise throat patch and long streamer tail are extraordinary.
- **Asian paradise flycatcher** — breeding birds at Sattal from April; the male's long white tail ribbons trailing through the forest are iconic.
- **Orange-headed thrush** — forages on the ground in the early morning.
- **Brown-breasted flycatcher** — a common winter migrant from the Himalayas.
- **Forest wagtail** — walks deliberately on the forest floor, rocking its body.

### Corbett (Terai Wildlife)

- **Bengal tiger** — Corbett's Dhikala and Bijrani zones have healthy tiger populations; the Ramganga river and the elephant grass of the Dhikala chaur (grassland) are the prime photography settings.
- **Asian elephant** — Corbett has large herds; the Dhikala grasslands frequently hold 50+ elephants at once.
- **Crocodile (mugger)** — basking on the Ramganga river sandbanks; close approaches by vehicle on the riverbank road.
- **Great slaty woodpecker** — one of the world's largest woodpeckers; Corbett's tall sal forest is one of the more reliable locations.
- **Pallas's fish eagle, grey-headed fish eagle** — over the Ramganga.
- **Crested kingfisher** — the largest kingfisher in India; the shaggy crest and dramatic black-and-white barring make it unmistakeable.

## Photography Notes

The three-destination circuit works best as a 5–7 day trip: 2 nights at Pangot for the Himalayan birds, 1 night at Sattal for the forest and lake birds, and 2–3 nights at Corbett (Dhikala or Bijrani zone) for mammals.

At Pangot, the forest road behind the village and the road toward Kilbury are the primary birding trails. Pre-dawn starts are essential for pheasants — they are most visible before 7 am.

Sattal's lakeside trees and the trails through the Nature's Valley resort area hold most of the key species. The forest is private land around here; use established birding trails with permission.

At Corbett, the Dhikala zone is only accessible with advance permits and requires staying inside the park at the Forest Rest House (highly sought-after bookings). The open Dhikala chaur at dawn — flat grassland, river beyond, and the Himalayan foothills as backdrop — is one of the finest wildlife landscape settings in North India.
