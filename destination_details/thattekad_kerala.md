## The Landscape

Thattekad Bird Sanctuary sits in the lowland forest of the Ernakulam district, between the north and south branches of the Periyar river. Salim Ali — the father of Indian ornithology — called it the richest bird habitat in peninsular India, and the claim holds up. The combination of moist deciduous forest, riverine vegetation, rubber and teak plantations at the edges, and the river itself creates a layered set of microhabitats that pack an extraordinary species list into a small area.

The forest floor is relatively open in the drier months, and many of Thattekad's most sought-after species are found low in the understorey or on the ground — making them more visible and photographable than comparable birds in taller canopy forest.

## Key Species

Thattekad holds several species that are reliably seen here but rarely encountered elsewhere in South India.

- **Malabar trogon** — a forest jewel; the male has a crimson-and-dark-green pattern with a long banded tail. Found in the shaded interior, often perched motionless for minutes at a time — a photographer's gift.
- **Sri Lanka frogmouth** — arguably the most-photographed bird at Thattekad. This extraordinary nocturnal bird roosts during the day on low, lichen-covered branches in dark ravines. Pairs are often in the same spot for weeks. A 200–400 mm and flash fill in the shade are standard.
- **Malabar whistling thrush** — the loud, human-like whistle along every stream; the glossy blue plumage with the shoulder patch shines in the right light.
- **Indian pitta** — a winter migrant (October–March) that is almost exclusively ground-level; its nine-colour plumage is among the most vivid of any bird in India. Patience on a trail edge will often get you within 3 metres.
- **Brown wood owl** — one of the larger owls; roosts in tall forest during the day, active at dusk.
- **Forest owlet** — a critically endangered species now known to occur here; one of India's rarest owls.
- **Rufous babbler** — an endemic babbler of the Western Ghats, social and active at forest edges.
- **White-bellied treepie** — a striking white-and-black corvid endemic to the southwest.
- **Malabar grey hornbill** — the Western Ghats endemic hornbill, common here.
- **Great hornbill** — present in the taller forest sections; Thattekad has both resident pairs and seasonal visitors.

## Photography Notes

Thattekad rewards slow, methodical birding rather than safari-style driving. Walk-based sessions with a local guide who knows the roost sites and territories of key species are far more productive than driving. The frogmouth roost sites, trogon territories, and pitta feeding areas are known to experienced guides and can be visited repeatedly.

Gear: a 400–500 mm telephoto is the main lens, but the close, low subjects here also reward a good 300 mm f/4. For frogmouth and trogon in deep shade, a monopod and high ISO capability (6400+) are more useful than flash, which can flatten the beautiful ambient-light quality.

The best photography months are October–March when winter migrants like the pitta, flycatchers, and warblers arrive. The forest remains green year-round due to the Periyar river influence, so the background quality in photos is consistently good.

The guides based around Thattekad village and the Birdwatcher's Jungle Camp have decades of knowledge of individual roost sites and seasonal patterns — choose your guide carefully, as the quality difference is significant.
