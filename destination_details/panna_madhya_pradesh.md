## The Landscape

Panna Tiger Reserve occupies the Ken river valley in the Vindhya range of northern Madhya Pradesh. The Ken river is the park's spine — a wide, crystal-clear river cutting through the pale limestone and sandstone plateau, flanked by tall forest of mahua, tendu, and mixed deciduous. The gorge sections of the Ken, where the river runs between 30-metre cliffs of pale rock, are among the most scenic landscapes in Central India.

Panna has an unusual conservation history: tigers were completely poached out between 2007 and 2009, and the current population was entirely rebuilt through translocations from Kanha and Bandhavgarh. The recovery to over 80 tigers within 15 years is one of the most successful rewilding stories in India.

## Key Species

### Mammals

- **Bengal tiger** — the population is healthy and growing, but the forest is denser than Kanha or Bandhavgarh and sightings require more patience. The Ken river corridor — particularly the section near the Hinouta and Madla gates — is the most productive zone. Tigers frequently come to the river to drink, giving scenic riverside encounters.
- **Indian leopard** — perhaps the most reliably seen large cat at Panna; the rocky plateau terrain and the river gorges are classic leopard habitat.
- **Sloth bear** — present in the rocky sections of the reserve.
- **Indian wild dog (dhole)** — resident packs.
- **Nilgai, chital, sambar** — good prey base.

### Reptiles and Birds

Panna's ecological distinction from other Central India parks is its **gharial and vulture** populations — the two most important wildlife photography subjects here.

- **Gharial** — the Ken river is one of only two rivers in India with a healthy breeding gharial population. These prehistoric-looking, fish-eating crocodilians have a distinctive elongated snout and the male's bulbous ghara (nasal growth). They bask in groups on the Ken's sandbanks from November to March. Close approaches from the bank are possible in sections of the river adjacent to the safari zones. A 500–600 mm from the opposite bank gives excellent full-body shots of multiple individuals.
- **Mugger crocodile** — also present on the Ken; often sharing the same sandbanks as the gharials, providing interesting comparison shots.
- **Indian vulture (Long-billed vulture)** — Panna holds one of the largest breeding colonies of this critically endangered species in the world. The cliff faces above the Ken river gorge are their nesting sites; nesting activity peaks from October to February. The sight of dozens of vultures spiralling on thermals above the gorge is spectacular.
- **Red-headed vulture (King vulture)** — the most dramatic Indian vulture; the scarlet head and massive build make it unmistakeable. Present alongside the long-billed vulture.
- **Indian skimmer** — breeds on the Ken sandbanks; Panna is one of the most accessible locations for this unusual bird.
- **Pied kingfisher, common kingfisher** — along every section of the Ken.
- **River tern** — nests on exposed sandbanks in the river.

## Photography Notes

The Ken river is Panna's strongest photography asset. A boat safari on the river (available from Madla and Hinouta) gives water-level access to the gharial sandbanks, the nesting vulture cliffs, and the riverside forest. The pale limestone gorge walls, the turquoise river, and the forested skyline above make every frame of the river inherently scenic.

For gharial photography, the mid-river sandbanks visible from the boat are the best locations. The animals bask in the morning sun and are indifferent to a slow, quiet boat approach. Shoot at river level for the most dramatic angle — the elongated snout makes the gharial a very three-dimensional subject and any height advantage from standing in the boat flattens it.

For vulture photography, the cliffs above Raneh Falls (just outside the park boundary) are an accessible starting point. The falls themselves — a natural canyon of crystalline basalt — are one of the most striking geological subjects in Madhya Pradesh and worth a dedicated photography session independent of the wildlife.

Panna is best combined with Khajuraho (25 km away) for a trip that adds extraordinary temple sculpture photography to the wildlife itinerary.
